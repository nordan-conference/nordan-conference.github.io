Robustness Under Distribution Shift

Assoc. Prof. Lin Zhang, Tsinghua University

Practical recipes for training and validating models in non-stationary environments: uncertainty estimation, test-time adaptation, and dataset curation strategies that minimize spurious correlations. We compare calibration metrics across shifts and discuss trade-offs between coverage and precision under constrained budgets.

Responsible Data Pipelines at Scale

Hanna Niemi, Director of Data, NordCloud

Building lineage-aware, privacy-first data platforms. Covers governance-by-design, reproducible feature stores, and policy-as-code to ensure compliant, high-quality datasets for ML. The session highlights practical controls for PII minimization and data contracts between producers and consumers.

13:05–13:35

Sustainable Computing for Scientific Workloads

Dr. Elias Korhonen, CSC – IT Center for Science

Strategies to reduce the environmental footprint of compute-intensive research: scheduling with carbon-aware signals, renewable provisioning, and efficiency metrics that balance speed, cost, and emissions. Includes a quick primer on measuring embodied vs. operational emissions in HPC clusters.

13:40–14:10

Designing With (Not For) Users: Participatory AI

Sofia Marin, Lead UX Researcher, People+AI Group

Field-tested methods for participatory design in AI products—from expectation setting and consent to co-creation workshops—and how to translate qualitative insights into model objectives and guardrails. Short case studies show how early user narratives averted harmful failure modes.

14:15–14:45

From Prototypes to Platforms: Operationalizing AI

Markus Voutilainen, Platform Architect, Helix Systems

Lessons learned converting successful pilots into reliable, maintainable AI services: SLAs, observability for data and models, incident response, and change management across product teams. The talk includes a checklist for post‑MVP hardening and a minimal service template you can adopt.

Day 2 — Applications & Impact

Friday, 13 March 2026
09:00–09:25

Public Sector AI: Safety, Equity, and Procurement

Helena Varga, Policy Advisor, GovTech Council

A pragmatic view on bringing AI into public services. We cover vendor evaluation, model risk tiers, human-in-the-loop requirements, and clarity around audit trails. Short examples include permitting, benefits eligibility, and emergency response.

09:30–09:55

RAG That Works: Retrieval for High-Stakes Domains

Omar Farouk, Principal Engineer, Arcive

Retrieval-augmented generation beyond toy examples: failure taxonomies, evaluation harnesses for evidence fidelity, and strategies to tame drift in document collections. We also compare hybrid retrieval, learned rerankers, and structured citations.

10:00–10:25

AI in Education: Feedback That Scales Without Losing Soul

Prof. Liisa Korpela, University of Tampere

Designing AI feedback systems that respect pedagogy, support diverse learners, and remain transparent. We look at rubric alignment, formative vs. summative roles, and when to slow down automation to preserve learning outcomes.

10:45–11:10

Healthcare AI: Beyond Pilots to Clinical Pathways

Dr. Marta Sokolov, St. Helen’s Hospital

Translating model outputs into action requires integration with clinical workflows, documentation, and accountability. We map common bottlenecks—alerts fatigue, explainability needs, and data interoperability—and share success criteria from deployed decision support tools.
